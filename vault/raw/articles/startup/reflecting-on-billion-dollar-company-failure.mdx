## Reflecting on My Failure to Build a Billion-Dollar Company反思我未能打造出十亿美元公司的经历

In 2011, I left my job as the second employee at Pinterest — before I vested any of my stock — to work on what I thought would be my life's work.2011年，我在Pinterest的第二号员工岗位上离职——当时我的股票还未归属——投身于我认为会是毕生事业的事情中。

Just had an idea for my first billion-dollar company. Tomorrow, I start building it.我刚想到了打造我的第一家十亿美元公司的点子。明天，我就开始着手打造它。

I thought Gumroad would become a billion-dollar company, with hundreds of employees. It would IPO, and I would work on it until I died. Something like that.我原以为Gumroad会成为一家市值十亿美元的公司，拥有数百名员工。它会上市，而我会一直为它工作直到离世。大概就是这样的想法。

Needless to say, that didn't happen.不用说，这一切都没有发生。

Now, it may look like I am in an enviable position, running a profitable, growing, low-maintenance software business serving adoring customers. But for years, I considered myself a failure. At my lowest point, I had to lay off 75 percent of my company, including many of my best friends. I had failed.现在，我看起来似乎处于令人羡慕的境地——经营着一家盈利、不断发展且维护成本低的软件公司，客户也十分喜爱我们。但多年来，我一直觉得自己是个失败者。在人生的最低谷，我不得不裁掉了公司75%的员工，其中还包括很多挚友。我彻底失败了。

It took me years to realize I was misguided from the outset. I no longer feel shame in the path I took to get to where I am today — but for a long time, I did. This is my journey, from the beginning.我花了好几年才意识到自己从一开始就误入歧途。如今，我对自己一路走来的道路不再感到羞愧——但在很长一段时间里，我却一直为此耿耿于怀。这是我的故事，从头说起。

## A weekend project turned VC-backed startup一个周末项目演变成了有风险投资支持的初创公司

The idea behind Gumroad was simple: Creators and others should be able to sell their products directly to their audiences with quick, simple links. No need for a storefront.Gumroad 的核心理念很简单：创作者及其他人士可以通过便捷简单的链接直接向受众销售产品，无需搭建店面。

I built Gumroad the weekend I thought up the idea, and launched it early Monday morning on Hacker News. The reaction exceeded my grandest aspirations. Over 52,000 people checked it out on the first day.我在想到Gumroad这个创意的那个周末就搭建好了它，并在周一清晨将其发布在Hacker News上。反响超出了我最大的期望。第一天就有超过5.2万人查看了它。

Later that year, I left my job as the second employee at Pinterest — before I vested any of my stock — to turn Gumroad into what I thought would become my life's work.同年晚些时候，我在 Pinterest 担任第二名员工期间，还未兑现任何股票就辞去了这份工作，转而将 Gumroad 打造成我认为会成为毕生事业的平台。

Almost immediately, I raised $1.1M from an all-star cast of angel investors and venture capital firms, including Max Levchin, Chris Sacca, Ron Conway, Naval Ravikant, Collaborative Fund, Accel Partners, and First Round Capital. A few months later, in May 2012, we raised $7M more. Mike Abbott from Kleiner Perkins Caufield & Byers (KPCB), a top-tier VC firm, led the round.几乎立刻，我就从一众明星天使投资人和风险投资公司那里筹集了110万美元，其中包括马克斯·莱夫钦、克里斯·萨卡、罗恩·康威、纳瓦尔·拉维坎特、协作基金、Accel Partners以及First Round Capital。几个月后的2012年5月，我们又筹集了700万美元。顶级风险投资公司Kleiner Perkins Caufield & Byers（KPCB）的迈克·艾博特主导了这轮融资。

I was on top of the world. I was just 19, a solo founder, with over $8M in the bank and three employees. The world was starting to take note.我当时意气风发。我才19岁，是一名独立创始人，银行账户里有超过800万美元，还有三名员工。世界开始关注我。

We grew the team. We stayed focused on our product. The monthly numbers started to climb. And then, at some point, they didn't.我们扩大了团队。我们始终专注于产品。月度数据开始上升。然后，在某个时刻，增长停滞了。

To keep the product alive, I laid off 75 percent of my company — including many of my best friends. It really sucked. But I told myself things would be fine: The product would continue to grow and no one far from the company would ever find out.为了让产品活下去，我裁掉了公司75%的员工，其中包括很多我最好的朋友。这真的糟透了。但我告诉自己一切都会好起来的：产品会继续发展，而且公司以外的人永远都不会知道这件事。

Then, TechCrunch got wind of the layoffs and published "Layoffs Hit Gumroad As The E-Commerce Startup Restructures." All of a sudden, my failure was public. I spent the week ignoring my support network and answering our customers' concerns, many of whom relied on us to power their businesses. They wanted to know if they should look for alternative products. Some of our favorite, most successful creators left. This hurt, but I don't blame them for trying to minimize the risk in their own businesses.后来，TechCrunch 得知了这次裁员消息，并发布了一篇题为《电商初创公司重组，Gumroad 遭遇裁员》的报道。一夜之间，我的失败公之于众。那一周，我没有理会身边的支持者，而是忙着回复客户的各种疑问——他们中的许多人都依靠我们来支撑自己的生意。他们想知道是否应该寻找其他替代产品。一些我们最看好、也最成功的创作者离开了。这让我很心痛，但我并不怪他们为了降低自身业务风险而做出这样的选择。

So what exactly went wrong, and when?那么到底是哪里出了问题，又是在什么时候出的问题呢？

## Failing in style 风格上的失败

Let's start with the numbers. This is our monthly processed volume, until the layoffs:我们先看数据。这是我们裁员前的月度处理量：

![Up-and-to-the-right growth chart](https://sahillavingia.com/0_z7tBf5i6wH-HbLhU.png)

It doesn't look too bad, right? It's going in the right direction: up.看起来还不算太糟，对吧？趋势是对的：在上升。

But we were venture-funded, which was like playing a game of double-or-nothing. It's euphoric when things are going your way — and suffocating when they're not. And we weren't doubling fast enough to raise the $15M+ Series B (the second major round of funding) we were looking for to grow the team.但我们是靠风险投资起家的，这就像在玩一场要么翻倍要么归零的游戏。事情顺风顺水时，那种感觉令人狂喜；而不顺时，又让人窒息得喘不过气。而且我们的增长速度不够快，没能筹到我们想要的1500万美元以上的B轮融资（第二轮主要融资）来扩充团队。

For the type of business we were trying to build, every month of less than 20 percent growth should have been a red flag.对于我们想要打造的这种业务类型，每月增长率低于20%本应是一个危险信号。

But at the time, I thought it was okay. We had money in the bank and product-market fit. We would continue to ship product and things would work out. The online creator movement was still nascent; the slow growth wasn't our fault. It always looked like change was right around the corner.但当时，我觉得一切都还好。我们银行里有存款，也实现了产品与市场的契合。我们会继续推出产品，事情总会解决的。当时线上创作者运动还处于萌芽阶段，增长缓慢并非我们的过错。那时总感觉变革就在眼前。

But now, I realize: It doesn't matter whose "fault" it is; we hit a peak in November 2014 and stalled. A lot of creators absolutely loved us, but there weren't enough of them who needed our specific product offering. Product-market fit is great, but we needed to find a new, larger fit to justify raising more money (and then do it again and again, until acquisition or IPO).但现在我意识到：是谁的“过错”并不重要；我们在2014年11月达到了顶峰，随后便陷入了停滞。许多创作者确实很喜欢我们，但真正需要我们这款特定产品的创作者数量并不多。产品市场契合度固然重要，但我们需要找到新的、更广泛的契合点，才能为进一步融资提供依据（并不断重复这一过程，直至被收购或实现首次公开募股）。

In January 2015, after our final double-or-nothing hail-mary, our bank balance dipped below 18 months of runway. I told my 20-person team the road ahead would be a tough one. We didn't have the numbers to raise a Series B, and we would have to work really hard over the next nine months to get even close. To that end, we deprioritized everything except features that would directly move the needle. Many were not core to our business, but we needed to try everything we could to get our monthly processed volume to where it needed to be.2015年1月，在我们最后一次孤注一掷的翻盘尝试后，银行账户里的资金只够支撑不到18个月。我对20人的团队说，前路会异常艰难。我们没有足够的条件完成B轮融资，未来九个月必须拼尽全力，才有可能接近目标。为此，我们将所有事项都放到次要位置，只聚焦能直接带来业绩增长的核心功能。很多功能并非公司业务的核心，但我们必须竭尽所能，让每月业务处理量达到预期水平。

If we succeeded, we would raise money from a top-tier VC again, hire more people, and pick up the journey where we'd left off. If we didn't, we would have to drastically downsize the company.如果我们成功了，我们将再次从顶级风投机构融资，招聘更多员工，然后从之前中断的地方继续推进这项事业。如果失败了，我们就不得不大幅缩减公司规模。

In those nine months, when the whole team knew we were fighting for our company's life, not a single person left Gumroad. From "this is gonna be hard," to "yep, turns out it was," every single person worked harder than ever.在那九个月里，当整个团队都清楚我们是在为公司的生存而战时，没有一个人离开Gumroad。从“这会很难”到“没错，事实证明确实如此”，每个人都比以往更加努力。

We launched a "Small Product Lab" to teach new creators how to grow and sell. We shipped a ton of features, including weekly payouts, payouts to debit cards, payouts to the U.K., Australia, and Canada, various additions to our email features, product recommendations and search, analytics to see how customers are reading/watching/downloading the products they've purchased, and add-to-cart functionality. And that was just between August and November.我们推出了“小型产品实验室”，教新晋创作者如何做大生意并实现销售。我们上线了大量功能，包括每周结算、借记卡打款、支持英国、澳大利亚和加拿大的打款服务，电子邮件功能的各类新增项、商品推荐与搜索功能、可查看客户如何阅读/观看/下载已购产品的分析工具，以及加入购物车功能。而这一切都发生在8月至11月之间。

Unfortunately, we didn't hit the numbers we needed.遗憾的是，我们没有达到预期的目标数据。

## Slim down or shut down? 精简运营还是直接关停？

Looking back, I'm glad we didn't hit those numbers. If we'd doubled down, raised more money, and appeared in the headlines again, there would have been a very real possibility of even more spectacular failure.回想起来，我很庆幸我们没有达到那些数字。如果当时我们孤注一掷，筹集更多资金，再次登上新闻头条，那么遭遇更惨重失败的可能性就会非常真实。

With that off the table, our options were:既然这条路已经被排除，我们的选择只剩下：

Shut down the business, return the remaining money to investors, and try something new.关停业务，将剩余资金返还给投资者，然后尝试新的方向。

Continue with a slimmed-down version of the company to aim for sustainability.缩减公司规模，朝着可持续发展的方向推进。

Position the company for an acquihire.让公司做好被收购式招聘的准备

Some of my investors wanted me to shut down the business. They tried to convince me that my time was worth more than trying to keep a small business like Gumroad afloat, and I should try to build another billion-dollar company armed with all of my learnings — and their money.我的一些投资者希望我关掉这家公司。他们试图说服我，我的时间价值远高于维持Gumroad这样一家小公司运转，我应该带着所有的经验教训以及他们的资金，去打造另一家价值十亿美元的公司。

I tended to agree with them, to be honest. But I was accountable to our creators, our employees, and our investors — in that order. We helped thousands of creators get paid, every month. About $2,500,000 was going to go into the pockets of creators — for rent checks and mortgages, for student loans and kids' college funds. And it was only growing! Could I really just turn that faucet off?说实话，我本也倾向于认同他们的观点。但我要对我们的创作者、员工和投资者负责——顺序便是如此。我们每个月都帮助成千上万的创作者获得收入。大约250万美元会流入创作者们的口袋——用于支付房租和房贷、偿还学生贷款以及孩子的大学学费。而且这个数额还在不断增长！我真的能就这样关掉这台“印钞机”吗？

If I sold the company, it would be mostly for our stellar team — and I would no longer be able to control the destiny of the product. There were too many acquisition stories of companies promising exciting journeys and amazing synergies to come — and ending with a deprecated product a year later.如果我卖掉公司，主要原因会是我们这支优秀的团队——但那样我就再也无法掌控产品的命运了。太多收购案例都是这样：公司起初承诺会开启精彩的发展之路、实现惊人的协同效应，结果一年后产品就被淘汰了。

Selling was certainly tempting. I could say I sold my first company, raise more money, and do this all again with a new idea. But that didn't sit right with me. We were responsible to our creators first. That's what I told every new hire and every investor. I didn't want to become a serial entrepreneur and risk disappointing yet another customer base.出售公司确实很诱人。我可以说自己卖掉了第一家公司，融到更多资金，然后带着一个新想法再做一遍。但这并非我所愿。我们首先要对创作者负责。这是我对每一位新员工和每一位投资者说的话。我不想成为连续创业者，冒着再次让客户群体失望的风险。

We decided to become profitable at any cost. The next year was not fun: I shrunk the company from twenty employees to five. We struggled to find a new tenant for our $25,000/month office. We focused all of our remaining resources on launching a premium service.我们决定不惜一切代价实现盈利。接下来的一年过得并不愉快：我把公司从20名员工缩减到了5人。我们费劲心思为每月25000美元租金的办公室寻找新租户。我们把仅剩的所有资源都集中到了推出一项高端服务上。

In June 2015, a few months before our layoffs, our financials looked like this:2015年6月，也就是我们裁员的前几个月，我们的财务状况如下：

- Revenue: $89,000 for the month 月收入：89,000美元
- Gross profit: $17,000 毛利：17,000 美元
- Operating expenses: $364,000 运营费用：364,000美元
- **Net profit: -$351,000 净利润：-351,000美元**

A year later, in June 2016, our monthly numbers looked like this:一年后，也就是2016年6月，我们的月度数据如下：

- Revenue: $176,000 for the month 月收入：176,000 美元
- Gross profit: $42,000 毛利：42,000美元
- Operating expenses: $32,000 运营费用：32,000美元
- **Net profit: +$10,000 净利润：+10,000美元**

It hurt, but it meant creators would keep getting paid. It also meant that we were in control of our own destiny.这很痛，但这意味着创作者能持续获得报酬。这也意味着我们能掌控自己的命运。

## From skeleton crew to lifestyle business从核心团队到生活方式型企业

It got worse from there. 从那之后，情况变得更糟了。

Gumroad was no longer the venture-funded, fast-growing startup our investors and employees signed up for. As everyone else found other opportunities, the skeleton crew fizzled from five to one.Gumroad 不再是我们的投资者和员工当初签约时所期待的那家风投支持、快速发展的初创公司了。随着其他人纷纷另寻出路，核心团队的人数从五人锐减到只剩一人。

I was basically alone. I didn't have a team, nor an office. And San Francisco was full of startups raising gobs of money, building amazing teams, and shipping great products. Some of my friends became billionaires. Meanwhile, I was running a "measly" lifestyle business. It wasn't what I wanted to do, but I had to keep the ship from sinking.我当时基本是孤身一人。既没有团队，也没有办公室。旧金山到处都是初创公司，它们筹到大笔资金，组建了顶尖团队，推出了优质产品。我的一些朋友成了亿万富翁。而与此同时，我经营的却是一家“微不足道”的生活服务类小生意。这不是我想做的事，但我必须让公司不至于倒闭。

Now, I understand some people would dream to be in that position. But at the time, I just felt trapped. I couldn't stop, but there was only so much I could do as an army of one.现在，我知道有些人会梦想拥有那样的地位。但在当时，我只觉得自己身陷困境。我停不下来，但作为一个孤家寡人，我能做的事情也极其有限。

I shut off the rest of the world. I didn't tell my mom about the layoffs — she had to read the article and tweets herself to find out. My friends were worried, but I assured them I was neither depressed nor suicidal. I left San Francisco for long stretches at a time, thinking that some travel would give me adequate distance. It only made me more lonely.我隔绝了外界的一切。我没把被裁员的事告诉妈妈——她只能自己去看文章和推文才知道。朋友们很担心，但我向他们保证自己既不抑郁也没有自杀的念头。我有很长一段时间都离开旧金山，想着出去走走或许能让自己彻底抽离。可这只会让我更加孤独。

Every day, I woke up and took care of all of Gumroad's support queries. I tried to fix all of the bugs I could. Often, I had to ask for help from former Gumroad engineers. They were all employed by then, but they always found time to help. Once all things Gumroad were taken care of, I tried to go to the gym, and if I had the willpower, work on a side project (a fantasy novel). Most days, I failed.每天醒来，我都会处理Gumroad的所有支持咨询。我会尽力修复所有能解决的漏洞。很多时候，我不得不向Gumroad的前工程师求助。那时他们都已另有工作，但总会抽出时间帮我。等把Gumroad的所有事务都处理完后，我会去健身房，要是还有毅力，就做点副业（写一部奇幻小说）。可大多数日子，我都没能坚持下来。

To me, happiness is about an expectation of positive change. Every year before 2016, there was an improvement in my expectations — in the team, the product, or the company. This was the first time in my life when the present year felt worse than the last.对我而言，幸福是对积极改变的期许。2016年之前的每一年，我的期待都会有所提升——无论是团队、产品还是公司。这是我人生中第一次感觉今年不如去年。

Living in San Francisco was already a struggle. When Trump won the election, I ended up leaving for good.住在旧金山本就已是一场煎熬。特朗普赢得选举后，我最终永远离开了那里。

## New beginnings 新的开始

Then one day, everything changed. Again. I'm wary about sharing this part of the story, because I don't know if there is anything to learn from it. But it happened, so here it is.后来有一天，一切又变了。我不太想分享故事的这一部分，因为我不知道从中能学到什么。但事情确实发生了，所以我还是讲出来吧。

On November 27, 2017, I got this email from KPCB, our lead investor:2017年11月27日，我收到了我们的主要投资方KPCB发来的这封邮件：

> I am following up our conversation a few months ago. KP would like to sell our ownership back to Gumroad for $1. Can we discuss this week?我想跟进我们几个月前的谈话。KP 希望以 1 美元的价格将我们的股份卖回给 Gumroad。我们本周能讨论一下吗？

Mike had left KPCB to start a new company, and KPCB didn't want the operational headache of appointing a new board member. Plus, it helped their taxes. In one fell swoop, our liquidation preferences (how much we would have to sell for before dollars started going to employees) went from about $16.5M to $2.5M. All of a sudden, there was a light at the end of the tunnel. Small, dim, and far away, but present. There was a path to an independent business, not beholden to the go-big-or-go-home mentality I signed up for when I raised money.迈克已经离开凯鹏华盈（KPCB）去创办新公司，而凯鹏华盈不想为任命新董事带来运营方面的麻烦。此外，这对他们的税务也有帮助。我们的清算优先权（即公司必须达到的出售价格，在此之前资金不会流向员工）一下子从约1650万美元降至250万美元。突然之间，我们看到了一丝曙光，虽然微弱、遥远，但确实存在。这意味着有一条通往独立经营的道路，不再受我当初融资时所接受的“要么做大，要么回家”的心态束缚。

One investor joined them. We've bought back a couple more, since then. I keep the rest of the investors up-to-date with a brief email every few months.有一位投资者加入了他们。从那以后，我们又回购了几位。我每隔几个月就会发一封简短的邮件，向其余投资者同步最新情况。

The future came into focus: I could grow a small team, slowly buy back our investors, and build Gumroad into a meaningful business focused on our creators. We would never become a billion-dollar company, and that started to feel okay. Certainly, the thousands of creators selling on Gumroad wouldn't mind.未来逐渐清晰起来：我可以组建一个小团队，逐步回购投资者的股份，将 Gumroad 打造成一家专注于创作者的有价值的企业。我们永远不会成为市值十亿美元的公司，而这一点也开始让人觉得没什么大不了的。当然，在 Gumroad 上销售产品的数千名创作者也不会在意这一点。

## Finding new forms of impact 寻找新的影响力形式

The eight years I worked on Gumroad were full of personal ups and downs. There were months where I worked 16 hours a day, but there were also some months where I worked four hours a week. Here's one way to picture that time:我在 Gumroad 工作的八年里，个人经历充满了起起落落。有几个月我每天工作16个小时，但也有几个月每周只工作4个小时。可以这样来描绘那段时光：

![Another up-and-to-the-right growth chart](https://sahillavingia.com/1_nRgMCKaG464-ma278ZG5pA.png)

Can you tell which is which? I can't. We had a sales team for a few years, then we didn't. Can you tell when we made the switch? I can't.你能分清哪个是哪个吗？我分不清。我们有几年有销售团队，后来就没有了。你能看出我们什么时候换的吗？我看不出来。

It doesn't matter how amazing your product is, or how fast you ship features. The market you're in will determine most of your growth. For better or worse, Gumroad grew at roughly the same rate almost every month because that's how quickly the market determined we would grow.不管你的产品有多出色，也不管你推出新功能的速度有多快。你所处的市场会决定你大部分的增长。无论好坏，Gumroad 几乎每个月都以大致相同的速度增长，因为这就是市场决定我们的增长速度。

Instead of pretending to be some sort of product visionary, trying to build a billion-dollar company, I'm just focused on making Gumroad better and better for our existing creators. Because they are the ones that have kept us alive.与其假装自己是某种产品愿景大师，一心打造一家价值十亿美元的公司，我现在只专注于把Gumroad做得越来越好，服务好我们现有的创作者。因为正是他们，让我们得以存续至今。

## Creating and capturing value 创造与获取价值

At a CEO Summit many years ago, my all-time hero, Bill Gates, took the stage. Someone asked him how he dealt with failing to capture so much value. Microsoft was huge, sure, but tiny compared to the total impact it has had on the world and on humanity.多年前的一次首席执行官峰会上，我一直以来的偶像比尔·盖茨登上了舞台。有人问他，如何看待未能创造出如此巨大的价值。微软当时固然规模庞大，但与它对世界和人类产生的整体影响相比，却又显得微不足道。

Bill's answer: "Sure, but that's true with all companies, right? They create some value and succeed in capturing a very small percentage of it." 比尔的回答是：“当然可以，但所有公司都是如此，对吧？它们创造了一些价值，却成功只攫取了其中极小的一部分。”

I am now more focused on creating value than capturing it. I still want to have as large an impact as possible, but I don't need to create it directly or capture it in the form of revenue and valuation.我现在更注重创造价值，而非攫取价值。我仍希望产生尽可能大的影响力，但不再需要直接创造价值，也不必以营收和估值的形式将其变现。

Take Austen Allred, for example. He's raised $48M for his startup Lambda School, and he got his start selling a book on Gumroad.以奥斯汀·阿勒德为例。他为自己的初创公司 Lambda School 筹集了 4800 万美元，而他最初是在 Gumroad 上卖书起家的。

[Replying to @Austen 回复 @Austen](https://twitter.com/Austen/status/961267089876004864)

I shifted my focus from selling my book (using Gumroad, the platform he built) to growing a VC-backed company (which he invested in). We still disagree on some things I’m sure, but everyone could learn a lot and quickly by getting out of his or her bubble.我将重心从售卖自己的书籍（借助他搭建的平台Gumroad）转向培育一家获得风险投资支持的公司（这也是他投资的项目）。我敢肯定，我们在一些事情上仍有分歧，但每个人都能跳出固有认知，快速从彼此身上学到很多东西。

Startups have been founded by former Gumroad employees, and dozens more companies have been massively improved by recruiting our alumni. On top of that, our product ideas, like our credit card form and inline-checkout experience, have proliferated across the web, making it a better place for everyone — including those that have never used Gumroad.前 Gumroad 员工创办了新的初创公司，还有数十家公司通过招聘我们的校友员工实现了大幅发展。除此之外，我们的产品创意，比如信用卡表单和内嵌式结账体验，已在网络上广泛传播，让包括从未使用过 Gumroad 的人在内的所有人都拥有了更好的网络体验。

While Gumroad, Inc. may be small, our impact is large. There is, of course, the $178,000,000 we have sent to creators. But then there's the impact of the impact, the opportunities that those creators have taken to create new opportunities for others.虽然Gumroad公司规模不大，但我们的影响力却十分深远。当然，我们已向创作者支付了1.78亿美元。但更重要的是这种影响力所带来的连锁效应，以及那些创作者借此为他人创造新机遇的机会。

## Opening up about our financials 聊聊我们的财务状况

I've found other ways to create value, too. After the layoffs, I didn't talk to anyone about Gumroad. Not even my mom. And after moving away from San Francisco, I felt pretty disconnected from the startup community.我也找到了其他创造价值的方法。裁员风波后，我没有和任何人提起过 Gumroad，连我妈妈也没有。而离开旧金山之后，我感觉自己和创业圈也相当疏远了。

As a way to re-engage with the community, I thought about sharing our financials publicly. Founders starting their own companies could learn from our mistakes, utilizing our data to make better decisions.作为重新与社区建立联系的一种方式，我考虑公开分享我们的财务数据。初创公司的创始人可以从我们的错误中吸取教训，利用我们的数据做出更明智的决策。

It was scary: What if we don't grow every month? It could scare off prospective customers. It's something I would never expect a startup seeking venture capital to do. It makes sense to hold those cards as close to your chest for as long as possible when you must raise money, hire people, and compete for customers with other venture-seeking startups.这太可怕了：要是我们每个月都没有增长怎么办？这可能会吓跑潜在客户。我从未想过一家寻求风险投资的初创公司会这么做。当你必须融资、招聘员工，并与其他寻求风险投资的初创公司争夺客户时，把这些底牌尽可能长时间地藏在心里是合情合理的。

But, since we were not any of those things anymore, it was easier to share that information. We were profitable, and a no-growth month won't change that. So in April 2018, I started to release our monthly financials publicly.但既然我们已经不再是那些身份了，分享这类信息就变得容易多了。我们当时是盈利的，一个月没有增长并不会改变这一状况。所以在2018年4月，我开始对外公开我们的月度财务数据。

Gumroad in April: GMV: $4.2M (down 2%) Revenue: $273K (down 2%) Gross profit: $65K (up 34%) Let me know if you have any questions!4月Gumroad业绩：商品交易总额：420万美元（下降2%）收入：27.3万美元（下降2%）毛利：6.5万美元（增长34%）如有任何问题，欢迎随时告诉我！

Ironically, more investors have reached out (we're just interested in raising money from our customers for the moment, thanks!), more folks want to contribute to Gumroad, and our shift in focus has brought us closer to our creators.具有讽刺意味的是，尽管有更多投资者主动找上门来（不过目前我们只打算从用户那里筹集资金，谢谢！），但也有更多人希望为 Gumroad 做贡献，而我们重心的转移也让我们与创作者的距离更近了。

And instead of freaking out about how "small" Gumroad actually is (like I thought they would), our creators have grown more loyal. It feels like we're all in this together, trying to earn a living doing what we love.而且创作者们没有像我预想的那样，因为Gumroad的实际规模“很小”而惊慌失措，反而变得更加忠诚。感觉我们所有人都在一起，靠做自己热爱的事情谋生。

Soon, we're also planning to open-source the whole product, WordPress-style. Anyone will be able to deploy their own version of Gumroad, make the changes they want, and sell the content they want, without us being the middleman.很快，我们还计划以 WordPress 模式开源整个产品。任何人都可以部署自己版本的 Gumroad，进行想要的修改，销售想要的内容，无需我们充当中间商。

In 2018, we donated over $23,775 (eight percent of our profits) to different causes. We raised money for the hurricane relief efforts in Puerto Rico and the floods in Kerala. We helped fund the Presence-of-Blackness project in speculative fiction, and a Mexicanx publication.2018年，我们向不同公益事业捐赠了超过23,775美元（占我们利润的8%）。我们为波多黎各的飓风救援工作和喀拉拉邦的洪水救援筹集了资金。我们还资助了思辨文学中的“黑人存在”项目以及墨西哥相关出版物。

## Seeking the non-binary 追寻多元无界

For years, my only metric of success was building a billion-dollar company. Now, I realize that was a terrible goal. It's completely arbitrary and doesn't accurately reflect impact.多年来，我衡量成功的唯一标准是打造一家市值十亿美元的公司。如今我意识到，这是个糟糕透顶的目标。它完全是主观的，也无法真实地体现个人影响力。

I'm not making an excuse or pretending that I didn't fail. I'm not pretending that failure feels good. Everyone knows that the failure rate in startups — especially venture-funded ones — is super high, but it still sucks when you don't reach your goals.我不是在找借口，也不是假装自己没有失败。我也不假装失败的感觉是美好的。所有人都知道创业的失败率——尤其是那些获得风险投资的创业项目——高得惊人，但当你没能达成目标时，那种糟糕的感觉依然挥之不去。

I failed, but I also succeeded at many other things. Gumroad turned $10 million of investor capital into $178 million (and counting) for creators. Without a fundraising goal coming up, we're simply focused on building the best product we can for our customers. On top of all that, I'm happy creating value beyond our revenue-generating product (like these words you're reading).我失败过，但我也在很多其他事情上取得了成功。Gumroad 为创作者将1000万美元的投资资金变成了1.78亿美元（且金额还在增长）。眼下没有融资目标需要达成，我们只专注于为客户打造最优质的产品。除此之外，我也乐于创造除了盈利产品之外的价值，比如你此刻读到的这些文字。

I consider myself "successful" now. Not exactly in the way I intended, though I think what I'm doing now counts.我现在认为自己是“成功”的。虽然并非完全按照我原本设想的方式，但我觉得我现在所做的事算得上成功。

Where did my singular focus on building a billion-dollar company come from in the first place? I think I inherited it from a society that worships wealth. I don't think it's a coincidence that Bill Gates was my all-time hero and the world's richest person. Ever since I can remember, I've equated "success" with net worth. If I heard someone say "that person's really successful," I didn't assume they were improving the well-being of those around them, but that they'd found a way to make a ton of cash.我当初一心想要打造一家市值十亿美元的公司，这份执念究竟从何而来？我想，这是我从一个崇尚财富的社会中继承来的。比尔·盖茨一直是我心中的英雄，也是有史以来世界首富，我觉得这绝非巧合。从我记事起，我就把“成功”等同于净资产。如果听到有人说“那个人真的很成功”，我不会觉得他们是在改善身边人的生活，而是认为他们找到了赚大钱的方法。

Wealth can be a measure of being able to improve the well-being of those around you, as seems to be the case for someone like Bill Gates, who has invested heavily in philanthropy. But it's not the only way to measure success, nor is it the best one.财富可以成为衡量你有能力改善身边人福祉的一个标准，比尔·盖茨这样的人似乎就是如此，他在慈善事业上投入了大量资金。但这并非衡量成功的唯一方式，也不是最佳方式。

There's nothing wrong with trying to build the next Microsoft. I personally don't think billionaires are evil. And there's a part of me that wishes I was still on that path.想要打造下一个微软没有任何问题。我个人并不认为亿万富翁是邪恶的。而且我内心也有一部分自己希望还走在那条道路上。

But for better or worse, I'm on this one now.但不管好坏，我现在已经参与这件事了。
